# Thesis

In common parlance and standard industry operations, the humans are not considered part of the fleet.
The correct structural relationship is Org → Fleet AND Org → Humans.
## Why Humans Are Not Inventoried as Part of the Fleet

* Asset Class: A fleet is strictly classified as physical asset inventory (machinery, capital expenditure).
* Labor Class: Humans are classified as workforce, labor, or personnel.
* Separation of Concerns: Drivers or pilots can switch vehicles daily. If humans were in the fleet, moving a driver would change the fleet's structural inventory, which disrupts asset tracking.

------------------------------
## Mapping the Hierarchy: Where "Fleet" Goes
To answer your question of how these fit together without treating humans as parts of machines, organizations use a dual-branch structure under the main umbrella.
## 1. The Standard Corporate View
The organization controls both branches independently, and they intersect only at the point of an assignment or task.

       [ Organization ]
          /        \
         v          v
   [ Assets ]    [ Personnel ]

       |            |
   ( Fleet )     ( Humans )


* The Link: A third layer—Operations or Dispatch—temporarily pairs a human from the personnel branch to an asset from the fleet branch.

## 2. The Alternative View (The Human-Centric Variant)
If you want a structure where humans are superior to or contain the fleet, the hierarchy shifts to a Departmental Model.

       [ Organization ]
              |
              v
     [ Operations Dept. ]
              |
              v
          ( Humans )
              |
              v
       [ Manages Fleet ]


* The Link: In this model, the organization hires humans (managers, dispatchers, drivers), and those humans run, maintain, and possess the fleet. The fleet becomes a tool owned by the human department.
