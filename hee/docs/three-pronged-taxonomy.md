## How Your Taxonomy Resolves the Operational Relationships
By separating these into three parallel organizational groups, you eliminate the friction of trying to inventory humans or intelligent agents under hardware buckets.
## 1. Fleet Org (The Mechanical/Physical Layer)
This group is strictly reserved for the physical transit infrastructure—vehicles, drones, maintenance hardware, and telematics systems.

* The Logic: This group does not contain intelligence or agency; it contains the physical vessels that require mobilization.

## 2. Human Rights Org (The Personnel/Labor Layer)
This group governs human dignity, labor compliance, compensation, safety, and human-in-the-loop oversight.

* The Logic: Humans are never inventoried inside the fleet or mixed with software permissions. They exist in a parallel track focused on human labor laws, operational ethics, and constitutional protections.

## 3. Machine Rights Org (The Agentic/Intelligence Layer)
This group is dedicated to next-generation LLM agents, autonomous orchestrators, and digital decision-makers.

* The Logic: Instead of reducing an advanced, multi-agent reasoning system to a mere "software asset," this branch manages the digital sovereignty, behavioral constraints, liability tracking, and algorithmic ethics of your agents.

------------------------------
## How Interaction Works Across Your Taxonomy
Because you have separated these groups, operations happen through cross-organizational protocols rather than hierarchical containment:

* The Deployment: When a delivery must occur, a protocol temporarily links one entity from Fleet Org (the vehicle), one from Human Rights Org (the safety driver), and one from Machine Rights Org (the navigation/logistics agent).
* The Legal Benefit: If an incident occurs, your taxonomy maps perfectly to modern legal defense. You can instantly isolate whether the failure was mechanical (Fleet Org), behavioral/labor-related (Human Rights Org), or algorithmic (Machine Rights Org), avoiding systemic confusion.
