# Thesis

Root node, per [`human-execution-engine#262`](https://github.com/Twin-Cities-Open-Systems/human-execution-engine/issues/262)'s
epic ("history as quant" -- building `hee-ratios(tm)` to compare
history->current<->future). Seed content, dogfooding
[`human-execution-engine#255`](https://github.com/Twin-Cities-Open-Systems/human-execution-engine/issues/255)'s
schema question with real prose first, before any structured `Kind:

exercise, not an endorsement of any side of it.

**Core claim**: Pierre-Joseph Proudhon's 1840 work *Qu'est-ce que la
propriété?* (*What is Property?*) is a real, dateable root node for a
lineage that later branches through Marx, Bakunin, and Kropotkin — and
the branches are substantive disagreements about property/authority,
not just personality conflicts between famous names.

## The root claim, more precisely than the slogan alone

*"La propriété, c'est le vol!"* — "Property is theft!" — is the famous
line, but Proudhon's actual argument in *What is Property?* draws a
real distinction the slogan alone flattens: he attacked **property**
(the right to the *income* generated by an asset without labor on it —
rent, interest, absentee ownership) while explicitly defending
**possession** (the right to control and use what you actually occupy
and work). That distinction is the load-bearing part of the thesis —
without it, "property is theft" reads as a blanket anti-ownership
slogan, which is not what Proudhon argued.

## Branch 1: Proudhon <-> Marx

Marx and Proudhon had real direct contact in Paris starting around
1844, and Marx initially spoke favorably of *What is Property?* (in
*The Holy Family*, 1845). The relationship broke down over the next two
years. Proudhon published *Système des contradictions économiques, ou
Philosophie de la misère* (*System of Economic Contradictions, or The
Philosophy of Poverty*) in 1846. Marx's direct, polemical response —
*Misère de la philosophie* (*The Poverty of Philosophy*), 1847 — takes
its title as a pointed inversion of Proudhon's own.

## Branch 2: Bakunin enters both relationships, then splits from Marx

Mikhail Bakunin was in the same Paris milieu in the 1840s, knew both
men, and was real influenced by Proudhon's mutualism. Bakunin later led
the anarchist/collectivist wing of the International Workingmen's
Association (First International, founded 1864) in direct, sustained
conflict with Marx's General Council — a conflict that ended with
Bakunin's expulsion at the 1872 Hague Congress. This is the lineage's
first real branch point: one shared organization produces a Marx-line
and a Bakunin-line that go separate ways from here.

## Branch 3: Bakunin's collectivism diverges into Kropotkin's communism

Bakunin's own position — "collectivist anarchism" — kept a link between
labor contribution and distribution (collectively-owned means of
production, but earnings still tied to work done). Peter Kropotkin,
through the 1870s-80s, pushed this further into "anarchist communism":
full communal ownership *and* distribution according to need, not labor
contribution. A real, substantive second divergence, not a rebrand.
Other real names in this same branch worth a future node: Errico
Malatesta, Élisée Reclus.

branch point above should be locatable to a specific real event/text

something to paper over.
